About

I work in NeuroAI and computational cognitive neuroscience, with Dr. Tal Golan in the Brains and Machines Lab at Ben-Gurion University of the Negev. I came to it from psychology and cognitive and brain sciences, and my work combines human behavioral data, machine learning and model-recovery simulations. The larger question is how to compare models with minds so that competing ideas can be told apart — which experiments separate them, and how people differ in the way they see.

I have been a teaching assistant on courses in computation and cognition, in neuroimaging and in deep learning, and have mentored two undergraduates in the lab. I like building things with other people: a brain–computer interface that let a person with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) answer yes or no, and a headphone add-on designed to filter the sounds that set anxiety off. And I collect lines about the mind.

Research

NeurIPS 2025

When the Best-Fitting Model Isn’t the Right One (Research page)

Is the network that predicts human judgments best also the right one? We fitted 20 networks to 4.5 million human odd-one-out judgments, let each generate judgments, and asked whether the comparison would pick it out. It picked the right one less than 80% of the time: far above the one in twenty of guessing, but wrong more than one time in five, even with millions of simulated trials. That is a result for the task, models, noise calibration and mappings we tested, not a claim that every flexible evaluation fails.
