About

To ask whether a network sees the way people do, you fit a flexible linear mapping from its features to people’s judgments and check how well it predicts — but the mapping reshapes the network’s features, and that can let the wrong network win. When we let each of twenty networks generate judgments, the comparison picked the right one less than 80% of the time: far above the one in twenty of guessing, but wrong more than one time in five, even with millions of simulated trials. That is our paper at the Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS) 2025.

I work in NeuroAI and computational cognitive neuroscience. I started my PhD with Dr. Tal Golan in 2024, coming from cognitive and brain sciences and psychology, and expect to finish in 2029.

I have been a teaching assistant on courses in computation and cognition, neuroimaging and deep learning, and have mentored two undergraduates in the lab. I like building things with other people — a brain–computer interface that let a person with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) answer yes or no, and a headphone add-on for the noises that set anxiety off. And I collect lines about the mind.
